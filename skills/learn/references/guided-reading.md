# Guided reading

Goal: the learner reads (or walks through) one article and leaves with its argument, how it
connects to what they know, and one question to keep thinking about.

## 1. Get the text

- Fetch the URL, or read the local file. **Never reconstruct an article from its title or
  from memory.**
- Paywalled or unreadable: guide through what is public (title, standfirst, the post itself),
  label it "full text not read", and keep the session short. Never log in or use workarounds.
- Count length to estimate time (see SKILL.md §2) and split into 3–6 sections that follow the
  article's own structure (its headings, or its argument steps).

## 2. Opening (one box)

In the question field:

- **The question this piece answers**, in one line.
- **Structure**: the sections in order, one line each, with minutes per section.
- **Total time** (reading + discussion).
- **What to watch for** while reading: one or two things — a claim to test, a number to check,
  a term that will matter.

Options: "Start section 1" / "Give me only the conclusion" / "Pick another item".

## 3. Each section

Start with "Section k/n · ~m min". Then, in the question field:

- **The point** of this section in two or three sentences.
- **Why it matters** — for the article's argument, and for the learner (their work, a concept
  they are studying).
- **Connection**: link to a concept they already have (by name, and as a wikilink if the
  vault uses them), or note a new concept worth adding.
- Optionally one short quoted phrase (under 15 words) when the exact wording is the point.

Options: "Next section" / "Explain this part another way" / "I disagree / have a question"
(the last one leads to typing — that is fine, it is their own words).

If the learner reads the original alongside, tell them which paragraph range each section
covers.

## 4. Closing

- The argument in three lines: claim → evidence → so what.
- Where it is weak or one-sided, if it is (missing data, survivorship, a conflict of interest).
- **One take-home question** in the style the config asks for (`learner.takeaway_style`) —
  something the article raises but does not settle, ideally tied to the learner's own work.
- Record: a few lines in the source note under `concepts.my_notes_heading`; new concepts
  noted for the user's normal note-taking flow; mark the item read in the reading list.
- Then check SKILL.md §5: does this piece suggest improving the learner's own system?

## Pacing

If the learner chooses "only the conclusion", give the closing directly and offer to go back
to one section. If a section runs long, split it rather than writing a wall of text in the box.

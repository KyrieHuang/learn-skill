# How variable-rate loans reset (fictional article for the /learn example)

## The two parts of your rate
A variable rate is a reference rate plus a margin. The reference rate is set by the market,
usually following the central bank's policy rate. The margin is set by your lender when you
sign and reflects how risky they think you are. When people say "my rate went up", it is almost
always the reference part that moved.

## When it changes
Most small-business loans reset on a schedule — monthly or quarterly — not the day the central
bank moves. A cut announced in March may only reach your repayment in June. Read the "reset
date" line in your loan agreement.

## Fixing versus floating
Fixing swaps the uncertainty for a premium: the lender quotes a fixed rate that already includes
what the market expects the reference rate to do. If two cuts are expected, the fixed quote
already prices them in. You only win by floating if rates fall faster than expected, and you only
win by fixing if they fall slower or rise.

## Caps, floors and break fees
Some loans have a cap (the rate can't rise above X) or a floor (it can't fall below Y). Floors are
common and quietly cancel the benefit of cuts. Fixed loans often carry a break fee if you repay
early.

## A simple check
Take your balance, multiply by a one-point change in the rate, divide by twelve: that is roughly
how much your monthly interest moves per percentage point. On a 50,000 balance, one point is about
42 a month.
